## The most private moments are the oneswe'd never put in an app.

And for good reason. Every couples app is an account, a cloud, and a server full of your most personal data, sitting somewhere you can't see. The honest answer to "where does this go?" has always been "trust us, it's encrypted on our servers."

In this category, that has never felt like enough. A breach, a subpoena, an acquisition, a policy change — the data is still out there, on someone else's computer, tied to the two of you.

## How it works

01

### Tap Start, set it down

Place your iPhone nearby and start a private session. The screen dims so it stops glowing — you forget it's there.

02

### It listens for energy, not words

Papaya measures only how loud and how lively the moment is — sound and motion *intensity*. The raw audio is turned into a number on-device and thrown away in the same instant. Nothing is recorded.

03

### Get your illustrated recap

Afterward, a warm 30-second story of the night: your rhythm, your waves, a mood archetype, and playful highlights — narrated by a papaya. The kind of thing that starts a conversation.

No account. No cloud. No analytics.  
**Your relationship never leaves your phone.**

This isn't a privacy promise — it's the architecture. There's no account and no cloud sync, so there's no server holding your recaps. The microphone only runs during a session, and raw audio is reduced to a loudness number and released the same frame — it's never written to disk. The only network call Papaya makes is to check your subscription with the App Store.\*

\*One exception, in the name of full honesty: subscribing in Russia — where Apple's in-app purchases aren't available — uses a separate path that verifies your subscription with our server via Sign in with Apple. It sends an Apple token to confirm the subscription, never your session or recap data.

## Pair by QR. No account, no server in the middle.

Pair phone-to-phone with a simple QR handshake. A 256-bit key is generated on one phone and handed to the other; it lives only in each device's keychain, never synced to iCloud.

Anything you share with your partner is end-to-end encrypted and travels over AirDrop or Messages — the transport only ever sees ciphertext. Unpair, and the key is gone.

## Questions

Is it recording audio of us?

Not by default. The microphone only runs during a session, and the sound is turned into a loudness number and released the same frame — it's never written to disk. Papaya measures only how loud and how lively a moment is, never what's said. Two things you can opt into, both off by default: attaching a voice or photo memo to a recap (saved on your phone, deleted when you delete the recap), and an on-device conversation transcript (speech-to-text, encrypted, never uploaded — audio itself still never touches disk).

Where does my data go in the cloud?

Your recaps, reflections, motion, and any health context never leave your device — there's no account, no cloud, and no analytics. For most people the only network call Papaya makes is Apple's subscription check. The one exception, stated plainly: if you subscribe in Russia (where Apple's in-app purchases aren't available), a separate path verifies your subscription with our server using Sign in with Apple — it sends an Apple token to confirm the subscription, never your session data.

How does pairing with my partner work?

You pair phone-to-phone with a QR code. A 256-bit key is generated on one phone and handed to the other, and it lives only in each phone's keychain — never synced to iCloud. Reflections you share are end-to-end encrypted and travel over AirDrop or Messages, so the transport only ever sees ciphertext. Unpair and the key is destroyed.

Isn't this just a "tracker"?

Not in the data sense — nothing is stored on a server, there's no leaderboard, and you can't "fail." It's closer to a Wrapped-style recap: a warm, funny illustrated story that gives two people something to laugh at and talk about. The moment is the occasion; the conversation it starts is the point.

Do I need an Apple Watch?

No. Papaya works fully on iPhone alone. If you do wear a Watch, it adds optional, read-only heart-rate and recovery context — Papaya never writes to Apple Health.

What's free, and what's paid?

Free gives you 3 full recaps your first week, then one each week — the whole experience, not a demo. Papaya Pro ($4.99/month or $39.99/year) unlocks unlimited sessions, full history, trends, themed styles, and personalized insights.

Is there an Android version?

Not yet — iPhone only. A big part of the privacy story is leaning on Apple-native pieces: on-device ML, the keychain, and encrypted AirDrop handoff. Porting those guarantees to Android is real work, not a recompile.
