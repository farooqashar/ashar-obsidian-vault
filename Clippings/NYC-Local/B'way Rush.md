## FAQ

What is rush?

Rush tickets are discounted tickets available only on the day of the performance. Not all shows offer rush tickets, and the availability can vary from day to day.  
  
In-person rush tickets are purchased at the box office as soon as it opens. Typically, Broadway box offices open at 10 AM from Monday to Saturday, and 12 PM on Sunday. Some theatres (like the Hayes) may open at 10 AM on Sundays if there's a matinee performance. Be sure to check with your theatre before making plans. Rush tickets are sold on a first-come, first-served basis. For popular shows, people may start lining up hours in advance.  
  
For digital rush tickets, you can click on the links in the table above to access the ticketing platforms. For mobile rush tickets, you will need to have the TodayTix app installed on your phone. Clicking on the mobile rush links in the table above will open up the app for you.  
  
[History of rush tickets on Broadway](https://playbill.com/article/from-sleeping-on-the-streets-to-swiping-on-a-screen-the-evolution-of-rush-tickets-from-rent-to-digital-lotteries-com-361078)

What is lottery?

Some shows offer discounted tickets through a lottery system. While most shows accept lottery entries on the day before a performance, some open the lottery for the entire following week on a specific date. You can find the rules for each show by clicking on the links labeled "lottery" if you're visiting on your phone, or the links in the "lottery" column if you're visiting on a computer.

What is sro?

Standing Room Only (SRO) tickets are standing tickets, usually located at the back of the orchestra seating. These tickets are offered at a discounted price and can be purchased in person at the box office. They are only available when a show is sold out. The availability of SRO tickets vary throughout the day, as it depends on the show's sold-out status.

What is that chart with orange/blue dots in it?

This is the schedule of the show for the current week. I cannot guarantee these are always up to date, please check the official source when making plans.  
  
The 7-grid layout represents Monday through Sunday from left to right. Saturday and Sunday are indicated with diagonal lines in the background. Each dot on the grid signifies a show scheduled for that day. If a dot is in the top row, it's a matinee show; a dot in the bottom row represents an evening show. When you hover your mouse over the calendar, you can see the exact time of each performance.  
  
Orange dots indicate the usual Broadway schedule, which includes Tuesday night, Wednesday with two shows, Thursday night, Friday night, Saturday with two shows, and Sunday matinee. Any show time outside of that is marked with a blue dot for easy identification.  
  
On a computer or tablet, or turn your phone sideways, you will see a right arrow icon next to the header labeled 'WEEK OF XX/XX/XX'. Clicking on it will display schedules for the next few weeks.
