> "Since 2013, the judges have split over the rule on tenant protections in 13 housing cases, through a dissent or a concurrence. In all 13, Judge Jenny Rivera took the position that would give tenants more protections. That is more often than any other judge now sitting."

Until now, finding that out meant reading hundreds of decisions and comparing each opinion's rule by hand.

## What the Supreme Court is to the country, this court is to New York.

The Court of Appeals is the final word on New York law, including the state constitution. Its rulings settle everyday matters like divorce and custody, and questions of national weight like redistricting. The seats opening now will be filled for terms that shape New Yorkers' lives for more than a decade.

Scrutinize uses large language models to read every Court of Appeals decision since Judge Rivera, the longest-serving judge, joined the bench. That's more than 1,700 decisions, analyzed the same way, by the same rules, every time.

What once needed an army of lawyers with expertise in dozens of fields now takes a defined, testable process. Faster, cheaper, and continuous.

### Not one prompt. A pipeline.

Each decision runs through a chain of narrow steps. Some are simple, like reading who voted with whom. Others are real legal analysis: finding the rule each opinion states and asking how competing rules would move the law.

Accuracy is the guiding principle. When the analysis doesn't meet the bar, it doesn't reach the page. It stays in the full dataset, marked, for anyone to inspect.

Large language models change the economics of turning public records into public knowledge. What once took an army of specialists now takes a rigorous, testable framework. And the framework can be applied beyond the courts.
