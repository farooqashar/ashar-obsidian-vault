## Open source, so you can make it yours.

Deploy Cloudflare OS in your own account, connect it to internal systems, and shape it around your organization’s terminology, policies, and ways of working.

Cloudflare OS runs on [Workers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/), with agent code isolated in [Dynamic Workers](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dynamic-workers/). Control who can use it with [Cloudflare Access](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/access-controls/). Use any model through [AI Gateway](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/), with one place to manage routing and spend.

[View on GitHub](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-os)

## Capture how your company works.

Turn your terminology, procedures, and best-known ways of working into shared context and skills an agent can follow. When one person finds a better way to do something, everyone benefits.

## Keep agents isolated. Keep data private.

Every agent and app starts with access to nothing. Sandboxed code cannot reach the Internet or your systems except through resources you provide.

Gatekeepers hold credentials, enforce policy, record what was read, and mediate actions. [MCP Server Portals](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/access-controls/ai-controls/mcp-portals/) bring existing MCP servers under Access policy. Shared work stays limited to people who can access its sources.

1. 01
	### Strong isolation
	Each agent runs in its own sandbox with its own storage. It cannot use the internet or reach another app.
2. 02
	### Narrow access
	Gatekeepers grant access to specific resources, not whole systems. They can limit fields and actions, and require human approval for writes.
3. 03
	### Private when shared
	A person can use a shared app only when they already have access to the data behind it.
